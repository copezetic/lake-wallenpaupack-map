Boating rules on Lake Wallenpaupack

The lake's own boating rules in brief, and where to find fuel and a pump-out.

Open the 3D map

Speed

  • 25 mph at nightBetween sunset and sunrise.
  • 45 mph on summer weekend daysSunrise to sunset on weekends and holidays, from the Saturday before Memorial Day to Labor Day. The regulation sets no numeric daytime limit on weekdays or outside that season.
  • No horsepower limitSo says the Fish and Boat Commission's page for the lake.

Slow, no wake

  • Within 100 feet of shore, docks and peopleThe statewide rule: slow, no-wake speed within 100 feet of the shoreline, docks, launch ramps, swimmers, downed skiers, people wading, anchored, moored or drifting boats, floats, and anywhere so marked. The button above draws that 100-foot strip along the shore.
  • On Wallenpaupack CreekNamed in the lake's regulation.
  • Below the Ledgedale BridgeFrom the bridge to a point about 1,000 feet downstream.
  • By the damFrom the dam to a point about 600 feet south.
  • Behind Cairns IslandBetween the island and the Pike County shore.

Towed sports

  • Waterskiing at the western endNot allowed on weekends and holidays between a buoyed line about 200 yards east of Cairns Island and the Ledgedale Recreation Area.
  • Kiteskiing and parasailingNot allowed anywhere on the lake on weekends and holidays.

Fuel on the water

Only marinas that say on their own websites that they have a gas dock are listed. Others may sell fuel: call ahead, and expect docks to close for the winter.

  • Lighthouse Harbor MarinaLists a gas dock.
  • 1st Klas MarinaAdvertises a gas dock.

Pump-out

  • Wilsonville Recreation AreaThe Fish and Boat Commission says sewage pump-out is available here for a fee.
  • Caffrey Recreation AreaThe Fish and Boat Commission says sewage pump-out is available here for a fee.

Depth

The Fish and Boat Commission gives the lake as about 60 feet at its deepest. No openly licensed depth survey of the lake was found, so this map shows no depth contours.

    A summary, not the regulations and not a chart. Buoys and posted signs on the water come first, and rules change.
